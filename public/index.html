The idea

What does a spacecraft leave behind?

Six pieces of hardware, from a mirror the size of a suitcase on the Moon to a probe beyond the Sun’s influence. For each one: where it is, why it was built, what it taught us, whether it still works, and where to check. The mission may end. The evidence remains.

57years since Armstrong and Aldrin left the Apollo 11 reflector on the Moon. Observatories still fire lasers at it.
49years since Voyager 1 launched. It is still sending data from interstellar space.
83days Sojourner worked on Mars, against a plan of 7.
6years Spirit worked on Mars, on a mission planned for 90 days.
~15years Opportunity worked on Mars, on a mission planned for 90 days.
45km driven by Perseverance, a record on another world. It is still driving.

Still there is not the same as still working

Spirit and Opportunity are still on Mars, but they have been silent for years. Voyager 1 still works and sends data, but with only two of its ten instruments switched on.

Label every claimEstablished, interpretation or open question. Never blended together.

About

About SpaceEdu

A student project about what NASA’s explorers leave behind, and how we know whether they still work.

Why
Space missions end, but the hardware and the evidence stay. SpaceEdu shows what six NASA explorers left behind and whether each one still works.
For whom
School students who want to understand how we know what we know about the Moon, Mars and deep space.
Where the data comes from
NASA and JPL mission pages, NASA’s 3D models and photographs, the JPL Horizons ephemeris and NASA’s live Perseverance feeds. Every claim links to its source.
How the facts were checked
The team’s analyst collected the facts into one table on Oct. 22, 2026, from NASA pages only. Calculated figures are labelled as calculated, and the simplified 3D models as simplified.
The team
Team lead: Muzayana. Analyst, engineer and programmer: the team of four.
